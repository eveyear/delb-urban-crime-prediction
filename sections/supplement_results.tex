\section{Estimator Sensitivity and Training-Period-Frozen Decision Populations}
\label{supsec:estimator_domain}

The estimator-sensitivity analysis evaluated the plug-in, Miller--Madow, and
coherent observed-support Jeffreys--Dirichlet estimators across 16
prespecified specifications in each city. All 16 DELB-reduction point
estimates were positive for every city and estimator
(Table~\ref{tab:sup_estimator_specifications}). The Jeffreys calculation
places a symmetric posterior on observed joint states and coherently
aggregates its marginals; it does not reconstruct unobserved support.

The estimator-specific randomization analysis applied all three estimators to the same 999 temporal-block, spatial-series,
and circular-shift surrogates. Table~\ref{tab:sup_estimator_nulls} reports
both Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment within each estimator's nine
city--design comparisons and global adjustment over all 27 comparisons.
Figure~\ref{fig:sup_estimator_nulls} shows observed CMI relative to the
estimator-specific null mean.

\section{Sample-Size and Multiscale Details}
\label{supsec:moved_scale}

\subsection{Nested-domain sampling and normalization}
\rev{The comparison domain comprised complete 2~km parents, their four
1~km children, and all sixteen 500~m descendants. Crime and bicycle totals
reconciled across this nested footprint. Weekly records summed seven
complete days; a one-period lag referred to the preceding aggregation
period. Sample fractions retained all grids within selected blocks.
Daily panels used seven-day blocks and weekly panels four-week blocks,
stratified by year and season. Fractions were nested within each of 50
frozen repetitions; a five-point expanding-window design separately
diagnosed chronological accumulation and distribution drift.}

Estimator stability for an entropy, CMI, or DELB estimate was
\begin{equation}
S_N(\widehat\theta)
=\frac{|\widehat\theta_N-\widehat\theta_{N_{\max}}|}
{\max(|\widehat\theta_{N_{\max}}|,10^{-12})}.
\label{eq:e16_stability}
\end{equation}
\rev{This is deviation from the full-sample estimate, not error relative to
unknown population truth. For cell area \(A\) in km\(^2\) and duration
\(\tau\) in days, the deterministically scaled intensity target has}
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{int,MSE}}^{j}(A,\tau)
=\frac{L^{j}(A,\tau)}{(A\tau)^2},
\qquad
L_{\mathrm{int,RMSE}}^{j}(A,\tau)
=\frac{\sqrt{L^{j}(A,\tau)}}{A\tau},
\quad j\in\{0,B\}.
\label{eq:e16_intensity_normalization}
\end{equation}
\rev{Temporal blocks in the randomization analysis contained seven days or
four weeks; circular shifts were separated by at least 31 days or five
weeks. At fractions below 100\%, the lowest-index frozen thinning path
entered randomization calibration, while stability summarized all 50 paths.
Miller--Madow CMI, DELB reduction, occupied support, singleton share, and
effective degrees of freedom were recomputed after every transformation.}

\subsection{Truth-referenced semi-synthetic protocol}
\rev{Six flow-quantile-spanning grids were selected deterministically in each
city. Observed baseline and bicycle states were held fixed; current crime
counts were regenerated from a negative-binomial model. Conditional means
used 20-observation shrinkage. Raw method-of-moments dispersion estimates
were retained as diagnostics, but simulation dispersion was bounded below
by 2.0 because the finer-scale supports could not attain 0.12-bit CMI under
the bounded multiplicative mean-shift family at their raw dispersion. The
bicycle-state coefficient was calibrated by numerical enumeration so that
\(H(C\mid\mathcal S^0)-H(C\mid\mathcal S^0,B)\) equaled the target CMI. Both
conditional entropies were mapped through the same inverse integer-lattice
envelope to obtain population \(L^0\), \(L^B\), and \(\Delta L\).
All three estimators were evaluated on the same generated datasets.
Truth-referenced outcomes were bias, RMSE, relative RMSE for positive truth,
Monte Carlo standard deviation, and sign recovery. Power used Miller--Madow
CMI and exact baseline-state-matched permutation without replacement among
observations having identical baseline-state codes. The 12 high-resolution
cells crossed \(N\in\{150,365\}\), CMI \(\in\{0.03,0.06\}\), and three scales.
Plus-one upper-tail \(p\)-values used a 0.05 threshold. The 80\% minimum
detectable CMI was the smallest tested positive CMI reaching 80\% power;
linear interpolation between adjacent levels was reported as a grid-based
approximation, not an additional simulated effect level.}

\section{Alternative Specifications and Rolling Origins}
\label{supsec:moved_rolling}

{\color{red}\sloppy Table~\ref{tab:sup_revision_robustness} reports the additional
matched-reference sensitivities. The lower-cardinality specification replaces
grid identity with training-only crime-intensity quintiles. Calendar-month
and positive-flow-quintile specifications intentionally increase temporal or
bicycle-state resolution; neither exceeded its matched-reference center.\par}

\rev{The bicycle-state mapping changes the magnitude of the uncalibrated
Miller--Madow CMI point estimate. For zero plus a positive-flow median split,
the Washington, DC, New York City, and Vancouver estimates were 0.02934,
0.09247, and 0.08310 bits. The primary zero-plus-tertiles mapping gave
0.04316, 0.13137, and 0.14233 bits, while zero plus positive-flow quintiles
gave 0.07169, 0.19650, and 0.24360 bits. These values come from the frozen
alternative-mapping results; higher bin resolution alone is not calibrated
evidence of additional source information. The finest mapping's
matched-reference comparison is reported in Table~\ref{tab:sup_revision_robustness}.}
