\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the DELB Measures}
\label{subsec:discussion_rq1}

The DELB is an information-constrained MSE lower bound for non-anticipating
integer-valued prediction under a specified target, information set, forecast
horizon, and squared-error loss. It maps conditional Shannon entropy through
the inverse integer-lattice maximum-entropy envelope and therefore provides a
model-independent benchmark for every admissible integer-valued predictor.
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact_delb} states its relation to the infimum risk and the
conditions for equality. The predictor-dependent refinement and
two-component decomposition then separate residual error--history dependence
from error-distribution shape mismatch.

\subsection{Why Estimated Information Is Not Calibrated Evidence}
\label{subsec:discussion_rq2}

At the population level, adding side information reduces conditional entropy
by its CMI with the target given the baseline information, and monotonicity
of the inverse lattice envelope orders the corresponding DELBs. Estimating
that ordering from sparse nested states is a separate inferential problem.
The known-truth simulations showed that positive plug-in and
Miller--Madow CMI estimates can arise when population CMI is zero. Dividing
each baseline state into weakly occupied joint states makes the estimate
depend on sample size and support geometry; standard corrections reduce some
of this behavior but do not remove it.

Estimator sensitivity produced the same substantive caution. Plug-in,
Miller--Madow, and observed-support Jeffreys--Dirichlet estimators preserved
positive DELB-reduction point estimates across the prespecified
specifications, yet none separated the observed city-level CMI from its
reference distribution. The Jeffreys result regularizes probabilities on
observed support and does not reconstruct unobserved states.

A transformation-based reference must likewise be distinguished from
population CMI and estimator bias. Each transformation changes the data
structure, after which the statistic and its support are recomputed. Its
reference center may therefore be positive. The support diagnostics showed
that effective conditional degrees of freedom and transformation-induced
support changes tracked this centering. These associations are diagnostic,
not a universal bias formula. Valid interpretation remains specific to the
estimator, sample, support, and transformation design.

These results lead to the following evidence chain:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\text{population DELB/CMI}
\longrightarrow
\text{finite-sample estimate}
\longrightarrow
\text{estimator behavior}\\
\longrightarrow
\text{design-conditioned reference}
\longrightarrow
\text{realized prediction error}.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:discussion_inference_chain}
\end{equation}
A positive estimate describes the observed partition, a randomization
comparison evaluates a specified reference mechanism, and held-out
prediction evaluates a fitted model. The truth-referenced scale--sample
experiment further showed that sample size changes estimation error and
detection probability while the population DELB remains fixed. It did not
identify a universal sample-length or scale-free detectability threshold.

\rev{The lower-cardinality sensitivity makes the role of state design
concrete. Reducing the spatial partition greatly improved occupancy and
revealed a statistically distinguishable but very small New York difference.
Washington and Vancouver remained centered on their matched references. The
appropriate conclusion is therefore neither that all positive estimates are
artifacts nor that the primary magnitudes are confirmed: finite-sample
identifiability changes with the conditioning-state resolution.}

\subsection{Interpretation of the Bicycle-Mobility Results}
\label{subsec:discussion_rq3}

The bicycle-augmented state yielded lower conditional-entropy and DELB point
estimates in every city. The reductions were substantially smaller in the
complete crime-supported deployment populations than in bicycle-covered
areas, especially in New York City and Vancouver. Coverage is therefore an
integral part of the target population, beyond its role in missing-data
handling.

\rev{The primary grid-identity randomization analyses did not isolate a signal
specific to recent, geographically matched bicycle activity: no primary
city-level or multiscale comparison survived the relevant calibration, and
distant lags produced similar estimates. The lower-cardinality sensitivity
identified only the small New York difference reported above. Because the matched-reference experiments had
limited power for small signals, non-rejection limits source-specific
attribution but does not establish that bicycle mobility is conditionally
irrelevant.}

\rev{The coordinate-source sensitivity further limits a blanket reading of
the Washington, DC primary null result. Excluding station-reference-located
endpoints increased its estimated CMI and DELB reduction and produced
circular-shift separation in a post hoc analysis, while temporal and spatial
references remained non-separating. This dependence on endpoint provenance
and reference design merits independent validation; it should not be
interpreted as confirmation of a city-wide mobility effect. New York City
was unchanged under the source-only restriction, and Vancouver lacks the
source coordinates needed for that comparison.}

\rev{Coverage explains most of the contrast between the bicycle-covered and
deployment populations. New York and Vancouver covered only about one quarter
of the crime-supported grid--days, and coverage-weighted CMI closely
approximated the complete-domain estimate. The remaining differences are the
observation-share-weighted CMIs outside the frozen footprint. Bicycle activity
was observed there in 2022, but the decomposition cannot attribute a specific
share of those differences to that year or identify a causal mechanism.}

\subsection{Prediction Opportunity versus Model Realization}
\label{subsec:discussion_prediction}

The held-out results illustrate why a lower information-constrained bound and
lower realized error are different outcomes. Added information may enlarge
the theoretically available opportunity even when a finite-sample estimator
cannot exploit it. Conversely, a model may improve by using seasonal or proxy
structure without establishing bicycle-specific population CMI.

This pattern was visible across cities and model families. Estimated
DELBs declined in every city, but held-out improvements were selective, and
only two New York City models narrowed the Predictability Gap. Under
classification loss, every estimated augmented Fano floor declined while the
statewise modal classifier worsened as unseen augmented states became more
frequent. Neither pattern violates its lower bound.

The Predictability Gap functions as a matched accounting quantity whose MSE
and DELB components must use the
same target, forecast horizon, information set, evaluation sample, and loss.
A negative gap change means that the tested model did not realize the added
estimated opportunity; it does not invalidate the DELB.

\rev{The empirical decomposition further shows that residual error--history
dependence is larger than marginal error-shape mismatch for every evaluated
model. Closing the observed gap would therefore primarily require predictors
that better use the declared forecast-time state, although the decomposition
does not identify which new model will do so.}

\subsection{Scope, Limitations, and Transferability}
\label{subsec:limitations}

For urban analytics teams, the framework supports staged data-value
assessment before procurement or deployment decisions based on one fitted model.
Table~\ref{tab:practical_decision_framework} translates the evidence chain
into four operational questions: whether the estimated floor changes,
whether finite-sample support is adequate, whether the statistic separates
from design-matched references, and whether matched models realize a held-out
benefit.

\input{sections/table_practical_decision_framework}

Any subsequent deployment assessment should remain city-, model-, and
population-specific, retain a baseline model and frozen holdout, monitor both
\(\Delta\operatorname{MSE}\) and \(\Delta G\), and reassess benefits under
rolling evaluation.

Several limitations bound this application. Bicycle trips provide a
mode-specific activity trace with incomplete coverage of the ambient population;
coverage, adoption, operations, weather, and recording differ by city.
Crime records depend on reporting, classification, and jurisdiction, and
2020--2022 includes pandemic-related structural change. Aggregate
grid--day associations do not support individual inference or a causal
effect of bicycle activity on crime.

\rev{Comparable forecast-time weather fields were not available in the
frozen harmonized panel, so weather and daylight were not controlled. Adding
city-specific sources after inspecting results would weaken comparability;
this omission is reported as a limitation rather than addressed by a
partial-city analysis. Bicycle flow may therefore carry weather- or
daylight-related information beyond the coarse calendar state. Since those
variables can be obtained separately, the present estimates cannot establish
that maintaining a bicycle feed is preferable to using less costly
environmental data for the same forecasting decision. Annual CMI varied
across 2020--2022, especially in New
York and Vancouver, so the pandemic-era estimates should not be treated as
stationary or directly transferable to later operating regimes.}

The randomization designs do not preserve the exact mobility distribution
within every baseline state. The known-truth and power experiments cover
finite fixed-design families, and the support regressions are descriptive
rather than analytic corrections. Some fixed-support CMI targets were
unattainable; matched-reference power was limited for small signals; and
minimum-detectable-CMI interpolation is grid based. Spatial and temporal
aggregation also changes the target distribution and MSE unit, so the
multiscale results do not establish a universal scale law.

Future work should focus on three issues: partial pooling for sparse states,
randomization designs that better preserve the conditional mobility
distribution, and evaluation with broader mobility and environmental data.
The workflow is also transferable to demand, epidemiological, traffic, and
service-arrival counts when the target, information set, and calibration
design are clearly specified.

These implications concern forecasting-system evaluation, not operational
policing decisions. Any public-sector deployment requires separate fairness,
privacy, governance, and causal-impact assessment.

\rev{In particular, an information-value estimate should not by itself justify
procuring surveillance-oriented data, expanding data collection, or allocating
police resources. Procurement decisions must separately consider necessity,
proportionality, representational bias, community oversight, retention and
access controls, and whether less intrusive data can answer the same planning
question.}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We developed a finite-sample evaluation procedure that links conditional
Shannon entropy to an integer-valued MSE lower bound and assesses whether an
estimated reduction survives support diagnostics, randomization calibration,
and held-out evaluation.

\rev{In all three cities, adding lagged bicycle flow lowered the DELB point
estimate. The reduction was substantially smaller over the complete
crime-supported populations than within bicycle-covered areas, and the
observed CMI was not unusually large under any prespecified randomization
reference in the primary specification. A lower-cardinality sensitivity
identified a small calibrated difference only in New York City, demonstrating
that empirical resolution depends on state complexity.} Held-out improvements were limited to selected city--model
combinations.
\rev{A post hoc exclusion of station-reference-located bicycle endpoints
also changed the Washington, DC point estimate and circular-shift diagnostic,
so that city's inference is sensitive to coordinate provenance.}

These results show that a lower estimated prediction bound does not by itself
demonstrate source-specific information or practical forecasting value.
Data-source evaluations should report the estimated bound change,
randomization-based evidence, and held-out prediction gain as separate
results.
